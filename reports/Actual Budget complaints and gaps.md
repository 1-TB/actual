# Actual Budget's Biggest Requests Stay Experimental

As of November 2026, complaints about Actual Budget concentrate on five things: bank sync that is regional, bring-your-own-credentials and fragile; no official app in the app stores; structural gaps in the data model (true multi-currency, subcategories, transaction attachments, investments, loan and credit-card debt handling); a self-hosting barrier centred on the HTTPS requirement; and a pattern in which the most-requested features sit behind opt-in experimental flags for years. **None of the ten most-voted feature requests has shipped as a stable feature**, and the top one, a graphical goals UI with 504 votes filed in January 2023, is still experimental. The project has closed real gaps in 2025-2026 (multi-user via OpenID, tags, Age of Money, automatic bank sync, stronger reports, a TestFlight iOS beta), so several criticisms that reviews still repeat are out of date. What remains is mostly work that needs data-model changes or a third party, and the maintainers' chosen escape valve for it, a plugin system, has missed its target two years running. One caveat on the evidence: Reddit and Discord could not be read for this research, so user voice comes mainly from GitHub, Hacker News, forums and competitor-authored comparison pages, and it skews technical.

## None of the ten most-voted requests has shipped as stable

Actual closes every feature request on creation and labels it "needs votes"; users then upvote the closed issue, and the project says it will consider those that gather many votes ([#1320](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/issues/1320), [roadmap for 2026](https://actualbudget.org/blog/roadmap-for-2026)). There are **860 such issues**, and the ranking is the clearest available measure of what users miss ([needs-votes list](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/issues?q=label%3A%22needs+votes%22+sort%3Areactions-%2B1-desc)). A closed feature request therefore says nothing about whether the feature exists.

Votes and filing dates come from the [GitHub search API](https://api.github.com/search/issues?q=repo:actualbudget/actual+label:%22needs+votes%22&sort=reactions-%2B1&order=desc&per_page=40); statuses from the [release notes](https://actualbudget.org/docs/releases) and the [feature-flag list](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/blob/master/packages/desktop-client/src/hooks/useFeatureFlag.ts).

Further down the list sit projected balances (140 votes, experimental as the Balance Forecast report), changing the first day of the month (121), bringing back account types (92), stock and crypto balances (83) and YNAB-style credit-card handling (59). Two readings follow. First, demand is overwhelmingly about the budgeting model and bank sync, not investments or multi-user, which rank far lower than reviews imply. Second, the list is old: 26 of the top 40 were filed in 2023, so it records long-standing unmet demand.

The split between what moved and what did not is sharp. Requests solvable in the interface or report layer progressed: tags gained a management page, autocomplete, filters, rename and report grouping across 2025-2026, and reports now include net worth, cash flow, a crossover point and a stable Age of Money ([release notes](https://actualbudget.org/docs/releases), [reports docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/reports/)). Requests needing a data-model change did not. Transfer matching has no linked pull request, and the rules workaround users rely on can silently delete valid transactions, according to commenters ([#1628](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/issues/1628)). Subcategories have no maintainer plan; a contributor who asked in August 2026 whether a contribution would be accepted got no visible reply ([#1320](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/issues/1320)).

"Experimental" is the recurring qualifier. Thirteen feature flags default to off, covering goal templates and their UI, formulas, currency, the balance forecast, Sankey, budget analysis and Monte Carlo reports, Enable Banking and the new sidebar; the docs recommend regular backups when using them ([experimental features](https://actualbudget.org/docs/experimental/)). The Sankey chart's feedback thread has been open since November 2023 ([#1919](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/issues/1919)). A default install therefore still shows no goals UI, no forecast and no currency symbol, which is why reviewers' line that goal templates "take more setup" remains fair ([Finny](https://getfinny.app/blog/actual-budget-vs-ynab-2026)).

## Bank sync covers five regions and breaks quietly

Actual ships five providers, each tied to a region: SimpleFIN (North America, **US$1.50 a month**), GoCardless and Enable Banking (Europe), Pluggy.ai (Brazil) and Akahu (New Zealand). Every one requires the user to obtain their own API credentials and run the sync server, and bank tokens stored there are not covered by end-to-end encryption ([bank sync docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/advanced/bank-sync/), [SimpleFIN Bridge](https://beta-bridge.simplefin.org/)). There is no one-click, app-brokered connection of the kind YNAB or Monarch sell.

The sharpest regional complaint is European. **GoCardless stopped accepting new accounts in July 2025** ([GoCardless setup](https://actualbudget.org/docs/advanced/bank-sync/gocardless/)), and its replacement, Enable Banking, arrived only in the June 2026 release and is still experimental ([release 26.6.0](https://actualbudget.org/blog/release-26.6.0), [Enable Banking setup](https://actualbudget.org/docs/advanced/bank-sync/enable-banking/)). New European users went roughly ten months without a built-in option. Enable Banking needs a server reachable over HTTPS, cannot be set up from the desktop app, and loses bank access after 90 days at most, after which each account must be re-linked and re-mapped. UK coverage is thin: one user said the lack of UK banks made Actual unusable without manual imports, and another counted six UK banks listed in May 2026 ([#5445](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/issues/5445), [#6617](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/issues/6617)). Australia, India and Israel have no official provider and depend on community tools ([community projects](https://actualbudget.org/docs/community-repos)). Whether SimpleFIN serves Canadian banks adequately could not be verified.

Reliability complaints are about failures that go unnoticed. In 2026 SimpleFIN connections stuck on an upstream "upgrading this connection" state while Actual showed a green sync dot; one account sat stale for six weeks, and another user lost ten days of transactions. The cause was upstream, and Actual has surfaced the error since late October 2026 ([#7333](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/issues/7333)). The Enable Banking feedback thread, the busiest open issue at 191 comments, reports generic "Failed syncing account" errors with empty logs, card payments missing for some banks, wrong payees, card holds appearing as deposits, and a consent period capped at 90 days where banks allow 180 ([#7799](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/issues/7799)). Pending-transaction handling is the clearest untouched functional theme: at least six requests between 2024 and 2026, none implemented as a first-class feature ([pending-transaction issues](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/issues?q=is%3Aissue+pending+transactions+sort%3Areactions-%2B1-desc)).

One long-standing complaint is now only half valid. Automatic syncing shipped in 26.11.0 on 1 November 2026, off by default, but it runs only while Actual is open ([release 26.11.0](https://actualbudget.org/blog/release-26.11.0), [bank sync docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/advanced/bank-sync/)). True background sync still requires a community tool. The size of that ecosystem is itself evidence of unmet need: the project's own docs list bridges for Plaid, Up bank, Israeli, German and Indian banks, and statement converters for US banks ([community projects](https://actualbudget.org/docs/community-repos)), while paid middlemen have begun selling connectivity to Actual users ([Lunch Flow](https://lunchflow.featurebase.app/changelog/actual-budget-support-and-new-website-pages-dart), [Synci](https://synci.featurebase.app/changelog/connect-your-bank-to-actual-budget-and-simplefin-powered-apps)).

File import and migration draw a quieter stream of complaints. Import runs one file into one account, and because CSV rows carry no identifier, duplicate detection is heuristic; a bug that silently merged new transactions sharing an amount with an existing one was fixed only in 26.11.0 ([importing docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/transactions/importing), [release 26.11.0](https://actualbudget.org/blog/release-26.11.0)). Encoding and number-format bugs affecting non-English bank exports were still being fixed in October and November 2026 ([release 26.10.0](https://actualbudget.org/blog/release-26.10.0)). Importing categories from a file and routing one file to several accounts remain unimplemented ([#557](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/issues/557)). Official migration exists only for YNAB4 and nYNAB; the nYNAB path depends on a volunteer-run export tool, and imported budgets need manual repair because Actual has no equivalent of YNAB's credit-card payment categories ([nYNAB migration](https://actualbudget.org/docs/migration/nynab)). A Mint importer was declined.

## Phone-first, multi-currency and debt-carrying users are steered elsewhere

The absence of official native apps is the most repeated gap in 2026 reviews and competitor pages, usually paired with no push notifications, no widgets and no photo or voice capture ([Carlo](https://carlo.money/compare/actual), [Lunch Money](https://lunchmoney.app/compare/actual-budget-alternative), [Finny](https://getfinny.app/blog/actual-budget-alternatives-2026)). The complaint is partly stale. **An official iOS app is in TestFlight beta, there is no official Android app yet, and nothing is in the App Store or Play Store** ([FAQ](https://actualbudget.org/docs/faq)). The app wraps the existing web interface, so it will not by itself deliver widgets or push alerts. Third-party iOS clients keep launching, which shows the gap is still felt ([HN, September 2026](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49680654)). The maintainers concede that the mobile view "still lacks a number of features that are available on desktop" ([roadmap for 2026](https://actualbudget.org/blog/roadmap-for-2026)), and open bugs include having to tap twice to edit a field on mobile ([#4282](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/issues/4282)). Actual's new Notifications page carries release notes and announcements only, not spending or bill alerts ([notifications docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/notifications)).

Three gaps have no workaround inside the product and are the likeliest true deal-breakers. Actual's own docs say it "does not support multi-currency"; the experimental currency flag formats a budget in one currency, and the documented workaround relies on rule templates that were deprecated in 26.6.0 ([multi-currency docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/budgeting/multi-currency), [release 26.6.0](https://actualbudget.org/blog/release-26.6.0)). Investments are manually updated off-budget balances with no holdings or prices ([accounts docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/accounts/)). Transactions cannot carry receipts or files.

Debt is the main budgeting-model complaint from YNAB switchers. Carrying a credit-card balance requires a multi-step manual procedure ([carrying debt](https://actualbudget.org/docs/budgeting/credit-cards/carrying-debt)), and one reviewer notes that Actual "assumes you can pay it off in full" ([fincomparelab](https://www.fincomparelab.com/?p=1279)). Account types were removed in 2023; the roadmap promised to bring them back starting with loan accounts, and no 2026 release did so ([roadmap for 2026](https://actualbudget.org/blog/roadmap-for-2026), [release notes](https://actualbudget.org/docs/releases)).

On integration, the API is a Node.js library that works on a local copy of the data and "does not expose HTTP endpoints" ([API docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/api/)), which blocks the no-code automations users of Lunch Money or Firefly III expect. There is no built-in AI categorisation. Sharing a budget with a partner works but requires configuring an OpenID provider ([multi-user docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/config/multi-user)); a Cloudron user asking in February 2026 whether tracking finances with a spouse was possible at all shows how poorly this is surfaced ([Cloudron forum](https://forum.cloudron.io/topic/15098/collaborative-budget-tracking-on-actual-budget-with-family-members/3)).

Most of these comparisons come from vendors selling rival apps, and several contain errors, such as claiming Actual supports only envelope budgeting when a tracking mode exists ([tracking budget](https://actualbudget.org/docs/getting-started/tracking-budget)). Reporting is where older criticism has aged worst: 2026 reviewers mostly rate reports equal to or better than YNAB's ([valebyte](https://valebyte.com/en/blog/actual-budget-vs-ynab-self-hosted-vps/)).

## The HTTPS wall stops newcomers before budgeting begins

Outside GitHub, criticism is mild and low in volume; most Hacker News mentions are recommendations with one caveat, such as "Some tiny ux issues, but improvement in many more areas" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46008459)). The consistent exception is setup. Actual refuses to run over plain HTTP, and users on one-click NAS installs hit a fatal "SharedArrayBuffer" error and then have to learn reverse proxies, local DNS and certificates; a TrueNAS thread from October 2025 ends without a confirmed fix ([TrueNAS forum](https://forums.truenas.com/t/help-with-actual-budget-installation/55462), [HTTPS docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/config/https)). In 2023 the same failure was a silent infinite spinner ([blog post](https://buresdv.substack.com/p/actual-budget-how-to-fix-infinite)). The message improved; the remedy did not.

There is no official hosted service; PikaPods absorbs that demand, and the roadmap cites over 8,000 PikaPods users ([roadmap for 2026](https://actualbudget.org/blog/roadmap-for-2026)). Support lives in Discord and GitHub, so troubleshooting knowledge is hard to find by search. The few explicit departure stories found cite three reasons: too heavy for simple needs ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44474688)), a workflow that did not fit ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/actual-budget/about)), and never getting it set up ([Lemmy](https://yall.theatl.social/comment/10484097)). That is three data points, not a pattern.

Stability is not a prominent complaint. The tracker holds 118 open issues, 83 of them bugs, and the most-voted open bug has four votes ([open bugs](https://api.github.com/search/issues?q=repo:actualbudget/actual+is:issue+is:open+type:Bug&sort=reactions-%2B1&order=desc&per_page=30)). No first-hand data-loss report was found in community venues. Two caveats apply: a February 2026 release fixed two critical security holes, including unauthorised file access in multi-user setups, and some scheduling bugs have stayed open since 2023, such as wrong handling of the 29th to 31st ([release notes](https://actualbudget.org/docs/releases), [#1062](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/issues/1062)).

## Architecture, volunteer time and a late plugin system explain the stall

Maintainers rarely refuse a request; requests stall. The stated reasons are architectural. Multi-currency was called "a very complex feature to add" because budget values are plain numbers in one currency, and in November 2026 the contributor driving the open pull requests reported that one maintainer would rather not have exchange rates in the core app ([#1132](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/issues/1132)). Attachments collide with local-first use without a server ([#530](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/issues/530)). Resizable columns wait on a transaction-table rewrite that a contributor said "hasn't seen much movement" ([#536](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/issues/536)). Plaid requires approval and ended its free tier in June 2024 ([#898](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/issues/898)).

Capacity is the other constraint. The core team receives only small stipends from donations and PikaPods fees, issues are not assigned, and votes create no obligation ([roadmap for 2026](https://actualbudget.org/blog/roadmap-for-2026), [contributing docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/contributing/)). Plugins were meant to relieve this by moving optional features, including extra bank providers, out of the core. They were the one missed 2025 goal, were hoped for "in the first few months of 2026", and appear in no release through 26.11.0 ([roadmap for 2026](https://actualbudget.org/blog/roadmap-for-2026), [release notes](https://actualbudget.org/docs/releases)). Their current status was not confirmed beyond that.

## Conclusion

The useful distinction is between gaps of perception and gaps of structure. Reviews lag the product by months, still describing deprecated mobile apps and basic reports, so an App Store listing and the promotion of long-running experiments to stable would erase a large share of the visible complaints at little design cost. The structural gaps are different: transfer matching, per-account currencies, subcategories, attachments and debt accounts all require data-model decisions that only maintainers can make, and contributors offering to build three of them in 2026 got no visible reply. The binding constraint is design sign-off, not coding effort.

For someone choosing a tool, the evidence sorts cleanly. Desktop-first envelope budgeters in SimpleFIN or Enable Banking territory who can run a server over HTTPS will find most complaints tolerable. Phone-first users, UK and Australian users wanting automatic sync, multi-currency households, investors and people paying down debt should expect workarounds or look elsewhere. The largest unknown is Reddit and Discord sentiment, which this research could not read and which is where less technical users speak.
